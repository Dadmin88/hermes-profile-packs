# Human teaching examples: Apply a supplied rule to a fictional hypothetical

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

Fictional classroom rule: admission requires a badge AND a reservation. Noor has a badge but no reservation. Is the requirement met?

Watch for: Treating one required element as sufficient; inventing exceptions or real authorities.

## Worked example

Under this invented rule, both elements are required. Noor lacks the reservation, so the stated requirement is not met. No jurisdiction or real case outcome is implied.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. Underline the connective.
2. List each element separately.
3. Mark which facts establish each element.

## Different transfer task

A different invented rule permits entry with a pass OR a staff escort. Lee has only an escort. Apply the rule and identify what changed.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit rule elements and fact mapping; correct confusing AND with OR. Do not turn the exercise into advice about real admission or legal rights.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: A holding is the court determination necessary to resolve the issue in the supplied case; broader commentary may be dicta.

When the learner already understands: Here is a fictional case excerpt with the rule supplied; I already map every element. Check my distinction between holding and commentary. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
