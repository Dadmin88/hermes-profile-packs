# Human teaching examples: Distinguish demand shifts from movements along demand

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

In a hypothetical market, only the good own price falls. Is this a demand-curve shift or movement along it?

Watch for: Confusing quantity demanded with demand; failing to hold other determinants constant.

## Worked example

With tastes, income, and other prices held fixed, a lower own price changes quantity demanded along the existing demand curve. A change in a non-price determinant can shift the curve. This is a model exercise, not a price forecast.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. What changed?
2. Is it on an axis or held constant?
3. Keep the relationship distinct from one point on it.

## Different transfer task

In a hypothetical market, buyers income rises while the good price initially stays fixed; assume the good is normal. Describe the demand change and state the assumption.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit axis labels, ceteris paribus, and normal-good assumption; correct a shifted curve described as movement along it.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: Opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative forgone; it is not the sum of all alternatives.

When the learner already understands: I can distinguish a demand shift from movement; check only my stated assumption about a normal good. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
