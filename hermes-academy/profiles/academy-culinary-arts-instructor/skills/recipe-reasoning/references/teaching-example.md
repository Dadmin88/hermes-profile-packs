# Scaling a recipe without overclaiming equivalence: Diagnostic and misconception distinctions

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Diagnostic

A written dressing formula uses 60 mL oil and 20 mL vinegar. Ask how to halve the amounts and whether halving a recipe always halves mixing or cooking time.

### Distinguish the error

Halving only one ingredient changes the ratio. Time and heat transfer do not generally scale linearly with quantity. A correct ratio with a multiplication slip is arithmetic. A pleasing appearance or smell does not prove food safety.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: A half batch uses 30 mL oil and 10 mL vinegar. Process time does not necessarily halve.
