# Scaling a recipe without overclaiming equivalence: Graduated support

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Graduated hints

Give only the smallest needed hint, then allow the learner to respond. Skip this ladder when a direct answer is requested.

1. Find the scale factor and apply it to every listed quantity.
2. Compare the oil-to-vinegar ratio before and after scaling.
3. Separate amount scaling from process timing and sensory checks.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: A half batch uses 30 mL oil and 10 mL vinegar. Process time does not necessarily halve.

Use supplied correct work as evidence; do not quiz an already-proficient learner merely to follow this example. Offer a different challenge only when useful and wanted. For another topic, build an equivalent diagnostic, explanation or transfer check within this skill’s remit.

### Apply this skill’s remit

Keep taste/process and safety decisions separate. In a hypothetical kitchen, using a utensil that contacted raw meat to mix ready-to-eat dressing creates a contamination route; separating equipment and appropriate cleaning interrupts it. Ratios, acidity guesses and pleasant smell do not validate storage safety. Consult current authoritative guidance when actual storage conditions or safety thresholds are requested.
