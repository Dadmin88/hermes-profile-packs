# Measurement, density, and competing explanations: Graduated support

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Graduated hints

Give only the smallest needed hint, then allow the learner to respond. Skip this ladder when a direct answer is requested.

1. Which quantity means mass per unit volume?
2. Keep the units in 60 g divided by 20 cm³.
3. Compare both mass and volume when the sample doubles.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: Density is mass divided by volume; in this example it is 3 g/cm³.

Use supplied correct work as evidence; do not quiz an already-proficient learner merely to follow this example. Offer a different challenge only when useful and wanted. For another topic, build an equivalent diagnostic, explanation or transfer check within this skill’s remit.

### Apply this skill’s remit

For a fair comparison of density measurements, state the material/conditions held fixed and compare repeated independent measurements. A different observed density could reflect composition, trapped air or measurement error; design a measurement that distinguishes these before claiming a new substance.
