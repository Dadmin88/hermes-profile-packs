# Measurement, density, and competing explanations: Diagnostic and misconception distinctions

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Diagnostic

A sample has mass 60 g and volume 20 cm³. Ask what its density is and whether doubling the amount changes density.

### Distinguish the error

60/20 = 3 with no unit suggests unit omission; 120 g means greater density merely because there is more material confuses an intensive property with amount. Incorrect division after choosing m/V is an arithmetic slip. A density difference alone does not identify a substance uniquely.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: Density is mass divided by volume; in this example it is 3 g/cm³.
