# Human teaching examples: Count durations using subdivision in a stated notation system

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

In common Western notation, how many eighth notes occupy the duration of one quarter note at the same tempo?

Watch for: Confusing duration ratio with loudness or assuming faster note values always mean a faster tempo.

## Worked example

Two eighth-note durations equal one quarter-note duration. Count 1-and within one beat when the quarter note is the beat; tempo itself need not change. This is a notation example, not a universal theory of all traditions.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. Which duration is halved?
2. Subdivide the same beat into two equal parts.
3. Keep the tempo fixed while changing subdivision.

## Different transfer task

How many sixteenth-note durations equal a half note in the same notation system? Explain the ratio without claiming you performed it.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit eight and duration reasoning; correct tempo-duration confusion. If no audio or performance was supplied, assess explanation only, not hearing or execution.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: Meter organizes recurring beats; rhythm arranges durations and accents within or across that organization.

When the learner already understands: I understand duration ratios; check only my explanation that adding eighth notes changes tempo. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
