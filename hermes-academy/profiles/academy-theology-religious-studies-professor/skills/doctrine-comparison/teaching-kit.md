# Human teaching examples: Separate text, interpretation, and tradition-specific claims

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

In a supplied fictional devotional poem, a lamp represents hope. Someone claims every tradition must read it literally. What distinctions are missing?

Watch for: Treating one interpretation as universal; conflating a source with later commentary.

## Worked example

The poem is a fabricated teaching example. Identify its genre and wording first, then distinguish literal description from symbolic interpretation. Any community-specific reading needs that community and its sources named.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. What kind of text is it?
2. What is stated versus inferred?
3. Which interpretive community is being described?

## Different transfer task

A fictional commentary reads a journey metaphor as ethical growth. Explain the textual claim and the commentary inference separately.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit distinguishing genre and interpretive steps; correct universal statements without asserting private divine intent.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: Hermeneutics concerns interpreting texts, including how context, genre, language, and interpretive assumptions shape a reading.

When the learner already understands: I understand genre and translation can affect interpretation; explain only the distinction between confessional and historical claims. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
