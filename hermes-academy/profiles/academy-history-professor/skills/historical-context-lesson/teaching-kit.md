# Human teaching examples: Evaluate what a source can establish

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

A fictional 1840 factory owner writes in an advertisement that all workers are content. What can this establish?

Watch for: Equating a contemporary source with impartial truth; assuming bias makes a source useless.

## Worked example

The advertisement establishes what the owner publicly claimed. Its commercial purpose limits claims about worker experience; independent testimony or records would be needed. This is an invented source, not an archival quotation.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. Who produced it and for whom?
2. Distinguish claim from corroboration.
3. Name another source that could test the claim.

## Different transfer task

A fictional diary describes food shortages in one village. Explain one supported inference and one inference needing wider evidence.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit provenance and useful limits; correct the leap from one perspective to a whole population.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: A primary source comes from the period or activity studied; it is evidence whose value depends on the question and context.

When the learner already understands: Evaluate a fictional campaign leaflet that promises prosperity; I already distinguish propaganda from evidence of actual outcomes. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
