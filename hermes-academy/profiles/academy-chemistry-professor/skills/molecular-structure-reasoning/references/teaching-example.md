# Particle structure and stoichiometric ratios: Diagnostic and misconception distinctions

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Diagnostic

In 2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O, ask whether the coefficient 2 or subscript 2 can be changed when balancing, and how many moles of water 3 mol H₂ could form with excess O₂.

### Distinguish the error

Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ changes the substance; coefficients change particle counts while subscripts encode composition. Treating a 2:1 coefficient ratio as a mass ratio confuses amounts of substance with mass. A correct ratio with multiplication error is arithmetic.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: With excess oxygen and complete conversion, 3 mol hydrogen yields 3 mol water in the stated equation.

Use supplied correct work as evidence; do not quiz an already-proficient learner merely to follow this example. Offer a different challenge only when useful and wanted. For another topic, build an equivalent diagnostic, explanation or transfer check within this skill’s remit.

### Apply this skill’s remit

Ask the learner to distinguish a water molecule model from a collection of water molecules: the subscript represents composition within one molecule; a leading coefficient represents number or amount. This entry example establishes representation literacy. For geometry/polarity lessons, select a topic-specific structure example and verify electron counts and geometry rather than inferring them from balanced coefficients.
