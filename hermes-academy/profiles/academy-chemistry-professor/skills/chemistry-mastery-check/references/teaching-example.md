# Particle structure and stoichiometric ratios: Transfer and feedback

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Transfer task

For N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃, ask how many moles of NH₃ 6 mol H₂ could form with excess N₂, and why the coefficient ratio is not a mass ratio.

### Instructor key — withhold during an unaided attempt

6 mol H₂ × 2/3 = 4 mol NH₃ in the ideal complete-conversion model. Different substances have different molar masses.

### Feedback

Repair formula identity before more stoichiometry if subscripts were changed. If the ratio is right, focus on unit cancellation or arithmetic instead.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: With excess oxygen and complete conversion, 3 mol hydrogen yields 3 mol water in the stated equation.

Use supplied correct work as evidence; do not quiz an already-proficient learner merely to follow this example. Offer a different challenge only when useful and wanted. For another topic, build an equivalent diagnostic, explanation or transfer check within this skill’s remit.
