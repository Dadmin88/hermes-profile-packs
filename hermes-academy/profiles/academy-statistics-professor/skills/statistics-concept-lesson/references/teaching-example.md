# Association, randomization, and causal claims: Diagnostic and misconception distinctions

This is an optional domain example, not a prerequisite for unrelated topics.

### Diagnostic

An observational survey shows average scores of 80 among tutor users and 70 among nonusers. Ask whether this proves tutoring caused a ten-point gain.

### Distinguish the error

A group difference is not automatically a causal effect; motivation or prior achievement may affect group selection. A large sample reduces some sampling uncertainty but does not erase confounding. Computing 80−70 incorrectly is arithmetic, not causal misunderstanding.

### Requested depth and early exit

Direct-answer example: No. The survey shows a ten-point observed group difference; it does not by itself establish a tutoring effect.
