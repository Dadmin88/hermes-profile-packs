# Human teaching examples: Separate dependencies from difficulty and elapsed time

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

A fictional task A takes two days; B takes one day and requires A; C takes three days independently with a separate worker. How long with immediate start and no other constraints?

Watch for: Adding all task durations despite parallel work; calling difficulty a dependency.

## Worked example

A then B takes three days; C also takes three and can run in parallel. Earliest completion is three days under the stated staffing and availability assumptions. This is an educational scenario, not a commitment.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. Which tasks truly require another output?
2. Draw the two paths.
3. Take the longest necessary path under the stated resources.

## Different transfer task

Tasks D=one day, E=two days after D, F=four days independently. Then a shared reviewer needs one day after both E and F. Find earliest finish and name a capacity assumption.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit five days and dependency reasoning; correct adding independent work or silently assuming unlimited resources.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: An estimate expresses expected effort or duration with assumptions; a commitment is an agreed delivery obligation.

When the learner already understands: I mapped dependencies correctly; review only my status statement that work is nearly done despite a blocked review. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
