# Human teaching examples: Align evidence with an observable learning objective

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

Goal: learners can distinguish observation from inference. Plan: reread definitions and ask whether they understand. Does the evidence demonstrate the goal?

Watch for: Equating participation, confidence, or recognition with application; assuming one method fits everyone.

## Worked example

Use a short new scenario and ask learners to label an observation and inference and justify each. This directly elicits the target distinction; feedback can address the actual misconception.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. What must a learner do?
2. Does the task require that action?
3. Change the example so copying a model answer is insufficient.

## Different transfer task

Goal: learners can explain a fraction comparison. Design a brief assessment that elicits reasoning, plus an accessible response option.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit alignment and changed-context evidence; correct a task measuring vocabulary alone when reasoning is the objective.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: Formative assessment gathers evidence to adapt instruction; it need not be a graded test.

When the learner already understands: My assessment requires new-case application and explanation; review only whether extra reading load distracts from the target skill. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
