# Human teaching examples: Use hierarchy to support a visual intention

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

A fictional poster describes title, date, and footnote as equally large and equally bold. If the goal is quick event identification, what could improve?

Watch for: Calling personal taste an objective error; prescribing color without seeing it.

## Worked example

Given the supplied description, differentiate title scale or weight, retain a readable date, and reduce footnote prominence. This supports the stated reading priority. Treat unseen contrast or composition as unknown.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. What should be noticed first?
2. Which variable could establish that priority?
3. Change one variable and compare the intended reading order.

## Different transfer task

A fictional information card has three equally emphasized instructions but one urgent first action. Propose a hierarchy revision and explain its communicative effect.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit intent, observable evidence, and a specific variable; correct claims about unseen visual features.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: Visual hierarchy is the ordering of attention among elements; scale, weight, placement, and contrast can contribute in context.

When the learner already understands: I already established title hierarchy; review only my description of taste versus a readability issue. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
