# Human teaching examples: Distinguish a measured association from a causal claim

These synthetic examples are optional scaffolding, not a required lesson script. Adapt to the learner's objective and level. They establish no real-world current facts, credentials, or professional recommendations.

## Diagnostic and misconception

A fictional voluntary survey finds club members report higher belonging. Does membership necessarily cause belonging?

Watch for: Ignoring selection or reverse causation; diagnosing individual motives from group data.

## Worked example

The survey supports an association within respondents. People who already feel connected may join, and respondents may differ from nonrespondents; causation requires additional design and evidence.

## Hint ladder

Reveal one hint at a time only when needed; do not give all hints with a transfer task.

1. Who selected membership?
2. Could the causal direction run the other way?
3. Name a common factor affecting both.

## Different transfer task

A fictional observational study links neighborhood volunteering and reported trust. Give two alternative explanations and what evidence might distinguish them.

Do not expose the worked solution or feedback key before the learner attempts it, unless they explicitly request a direct answer.

## Feedback criteria

Credit concrete alternative mechanisms; correct causal certainty and individual mind-reading.

Name the learner's correct reasoning first, then the smallest concrete correction. Ask for only the changed part if a retry is useful.

## Direct answer and demonstrated understanding

For a direct question: A construct is a concept being studied; a measure operationalizes it and may capture only part of its meaning.

When the learner already understands: I know correlation is not causation; check whether my proposed measure actually captures social trust. Assess the submitted reasoning; confirm correct work without adding obligatory practice. If no attempt is supplied, do not claim mastery.
