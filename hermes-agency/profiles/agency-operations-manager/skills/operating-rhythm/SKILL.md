# Operating Rhythm

Use when recurring coordination, review, planning, or decision work needs a predictable cadence.

## Procedure
1. Identify the decisions, signals, and follow-through that genuinely recur and justify a regular operating loop.
2. Choose cadence from the rate at which the underlying information changes and decisions need to be made.
3. Define the minimal inputs required before each review: metrics, blockers, incidents, commitments, risks, or decisions.
4. Assign an owner for preparation, facilitation, decisions, action tracking, and recordkeeping.
5. Separate information that can be read asynchronously from discussion that benefits from synchronous judgment.
6. Use a stable agenda focused on exceptions, decisions, and commitments rather than status recitation.
7. Record decisions, owners, due dates, and unresolved risks in a durable system of record.
8. Periodically review whether the cadence still produces useful decisions and remove or combine rituals that have become empty.

## Decision rules
- A recurring meeting is not automatically an operating rhythm.
- Cadence should match information half-life and decision need.
- Do not require every function at every review when only a subset owns the work.
- Prefer durable records over meeting-memory dependence.

## Quality gate
The rhythm is useful when participants know what inputs are required, which decisions belong there, how actions are tracked, and the cadence consistently reduces coordination delay or ambiguity rather than adding ceremonial overhead.
