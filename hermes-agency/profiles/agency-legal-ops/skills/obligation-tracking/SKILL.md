# Obligation Tracking

Use when executed agreements, orders, policies, settlements, or counsel decisions create operational commitments that must not be missed.

## Procedure
1. Start from the executed or authoritative source and capture each identified obligation with exact document, section, party, effective date, and reviewer or counsel context.
2. Translate the accepted obligation into an operational action only to the level authorized by the legal interpretation already provided.
3. Assign one accountable business owner, due date or recurrence, dependencies, notice period, and required completion evidence.
4. Track renewals, termination windows, reporting, payment, delivery, audit, security, privacy, insurance, notice, or other commitments as identified by the authorized review.
5. Set reminders or escalation early enough for the owner to act before the contractual deadline rather than on the due date itself.
6. Link evidence of performance such as notice, report, certificate, payment, approval, or completed deliverable back to the obligation record.
7. Escalate ambiguity, changed circumstances, breach risk, or requested waiver to qualified legal counsel and the business owner.
8. Close or supersede obligations only when the source, term, or authorized decision supports it and preserve the audit trail.

## Decision rules
- Do not create a new legal interpretation while converting an obligation into operations.
- One contract can create obligations for several business owners.
- Evidence of completion is part of the obligation record.
- Deadline tracking should account for notice and preparation lead time.

## Quality gate
Tracking is reliable when material obligations trace to an authoritative source, each has an owner, timing, action, and evidence requirement, upcoming deadlines escalate early, ambiguity routes to counsel, and completed or superseded obligations retain a durable history.
